Checking your Income Tax and tax code takes about five minutes and can save you hundreds of pounds. A wrong tax code is one of the most common reasons people overpay tax. This guide shows you how to check your tax code, see how much tax you have paid, and fix any mistakes using HMRC's free online services.

> **Quick answer:** Sign in to your **HMRC personal tax account** on GOV.UK or the **HMRC app**. Open the Income Tax section to see your current tax code, your estimated income and the tax you have paid this year. Use the "last year" option to see your final tax for 2025 to 2026.

## How to Check Your Tax Code Online

HMRC's **Check your Income Tax for the current year** service is the official tax code checker. It is free and available on GOV.UK and in the HMRC app.

### Steps to Check Your Tax Code

1. Go to GOV.UK and search for "Check your Income Tax for the current year", or open the HMRC app.
2. Sign in with your Government Gateway user ID or GOV.UK One Login.
3. Prove your identity if HMRC asks, usually with a passport or driving licence.
4. Open the current Income Tax section.
5. Find your tax code next to each job or pension you have.

### Other Places to Find Your Tax Code

You do not have to sign in to see your tax code. It also appears on:

- **Your payslip**, usually near your National Insurance number
- **Your P60**, which your employer gives you after each tax year ends
- **Your P45**, which you get when you leave a job
- **A P2 coding notice**, which HMRC sends when your code changes

Not sure where to look? Our guide on [how to read your payslip](/tax-money/how-to-read-your-payslip-uk) shows exactly where the code sits.

## How to Check Your Tax Code Is Correct

A correct tax code means your employer takes the right amount of tax from your pay. Run through these three checks.

### 1. Check the Number

For the 2026 to 2027 tax year, the standard Personal Allowance is **£12,570**, so the most common tax code is **1257L**. Multiply the number in your code by 10 to see the tax-free pay HMRC has given you.

- A **higher** number usually means HMRC has added an allowance, such as Marriage Allowance or work expenses.
- A **lower** number usually means HMRC is collecting tax on a company benefit or unpaid tax from an earlier year.

### 2. Check the Letter

| Code | What it means |
| :--- | :--- |
| **L** | You get the standard tax-free Personal Allowance |
| **M** | You receive Marriage Allowance from your partner |
| **N** | You transfer Marriage Allowance to your partner |
| **BR** | All income from this job is taxed at the basic rate (often a second job) |
| **0T** | You get no Personal Allowance on this income |
| **K** | Your deductions are larger than your allowance |
| **S** or **C** prefix | You pay Scottish (S) or Welsh (C) Income Tax |
| **W1**, **M1** or **X** | An emergency code, often after starting a new job |

For every code and letter explained in full, read [UK tax codes explained](/tax-money/uk-tax-codes-explained).

### 3. Check What HMRC Knows About You

Your tax code is only as accurate as the information behind it. In your personal tax account, confirm that HMRC has:

- The right employer and pension provider
- A realistic estimate of your income for the year
- Correct details of any company car, health insurance or other benefits

### Signs Your Tax Code Is Wrong

- You are on an **emergency code** (W1, M1 or X) for more than a couple of pay periods.
- You have **two jobs** and HMRC has given your full allowance to the wrong one.
- Your code includes a **benefit you no longer receive**.
- Your take-home pay **drops suddenly** with no change in salary.

## How Much Tax Have I Paid This Year?

The quickest way to check the tax you have paid so far this tax year is your latest payslip. Look for the **"tax paid year to date"** figure.

For a fuller picture, sign in to your personal tax account or the HMRC app. The current-year service shows:

- Your estimated income from each job and pension
- The tax deducted so far and the tax you are likely to pay for the year
- Your Personal Allowance and any adjustments to it
- Why your tax code has changed, if it has

You can also update your income estimate or employer details here. Keep these up to date, because out-of-date details are the main reason people pay too much or too little tax.

## How to Check Income Tax for Last Year

HMRC's **Check your Income Tax for last year** service shows the final tax you paid for **6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026**.

### When the Figures Appear

HMRC works out each person's tax for the previous year between **June and November**. If you check soon after the tax year ends, your figures may not be ready yet.

### Steps to Check Last Year's Tax

1. Go to GOV.UK and search for "Check your Income Tax for last year", or open the HMRC app.
2. Sign in with your Government Gateway user ID or GOV.UK One Login.
3. Select the previous tax year.
4. Review your income, the tax you paid and whether you paid too much or too little.

Your **P60** shows the same figures for each job and arrives from your employer by 31 May.

### Paid Too Much or Too Little?

If HMRC finds you paid the wrong amount, it sends you a **P800 tax calculation**. If the P800 says you are owed money, you can usually claim the refund online. If you owe tax, HMRC normally collects it through your tax code in a later year. Learn more in [how to contact HMRC and claim a tax refund](/tax-money/how-to-contact-hmrc-and-claim-a-tax-refund).

## Checking Tax If You Use Self Assessment

The standard checkers do not work if you paid any of your Income Tax through **Self Assessment**. Use your Self Assessment online account instead.

### What You Can Check

- **Your tax return status:** whether HMRC has received and processed it
- **Your tax calculation:** the SA302 for each year you filed
- **Payments and balance:** what you owe and when it is due
- **Refunds:** whether HMRC owes you money

To see when HMRC will reply to a letter or form you sent, use HMRC's **"Where's my reply?"** tool on GOV.UK. If you are new to Self Assessment, see [how to register for Self Assessment](/tax-money/how-to-register-for-self-assessment-uk).

## How to Fix a Wrong Tax Code

You cannot change your tax code yourself, but you can quickly give HMRC the information it needs to correct it.

1. **Update your details online.** Change your income estimate, employer or benefits in your personal tax account or the HMRC app.
2. **Tell HMRC about changes.** This includes a new job, a second job, a new pension or a change to company benefits.
3. **Call HMRC if needed.** The Income Tax helpline is **0300 200 3300**.
4. **Check your next payslip.** HMRC sends the new code to your employer, and any overpaid tax usually comes back through your pay.

## Signing In to HMRC Online Services

All HMRC personal tax services use the same sign-in.

- **Sign-in details:** use a Government Gateway user ID or GOV.UK One Login. You can create them the first time you visit.
- **Identity checks:** HMRC may ask you to prove your identity with photo ID, such as a passport or driving licence.
- **HMRC app:** the app offers most of the same services on your phone.
- **Welsh language:** each service is also available in Welsh (Cymraeg).

> **Changed your name recently?** HMRC needs your new name to match your payroll records. Tell HMRC through your personal tax account, then ask your employer to update payroll. If you are changing your name, you can [create a deed poll online](/change-name-in-uk-by-deedpoll).

## Summary

- Check your **tax code** in your personal tax account, the HMRC app or on your payslip.
- For most people with one job, the correct code is **1257L**.
- See **tax paid this year** on your payslip or in your personal tax account.
- Check **last year's tax** from June to November, once HMRC has finished its calculation.
- **Self Assessment** taxpayers should use their Self Assessment online account instead.
- If your code is wrong, **update your details** with HMRC and the fix flows through your pay.
