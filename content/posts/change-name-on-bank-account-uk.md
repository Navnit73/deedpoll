# How to Change Your Name on a Bank Account in the UK

Your bank is one of the organisations you'll need to update after a name change, and it's one of the most important. Your salary, bills, savings and credit history all run through your bank accounts, so a mismatch can cause real inconvenience. The good news is that UK banks and building societies deal with name changes every day, and the process is usually straightforward once you have the right documents.

This guide explains what banks accept as evidence, the different ways to apply, what happens to your cards and accounts, and how to deal with the issues that commonly crop up afterwards.

---

## Quick Overview

| Account or Product | Who to Contact | What Changes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Current account | Your bank | Name on account and new debit card |
| Savings account and cash ISA | Your bank or building society | Name on account, no effect on ISA allowance |
| Credit card | Card provider | New card in new name |
| Mortgage | Mortgage lender | Lender records; also update Land Registry title |
| Loans and car finance | Each lender | Lender records |
| Credit file | Updates through lenders; check with agencies | Reflects new name over time |

## Before You Contact Your Bank

### Update Your Photo ID First

Banks have to verify your identity under anti-money laundering rules. Many will ask to see photo ID alongside your evidence of the name change. If your passport or driving licence is still in your old name, some banks will accept it alongside your deed poll or certificate, but others prefer ID in the new name. Updating your ID first avoids any back and forth. See our guides to [changing your name on your passport](/how-to-change-name-on-passport-uk) and on your driving licence.

### Documents Banks Usually Accept

| Reason for Change | Usual Evidence |
| :--- | :--- |
| Marriage or civil partnership | Marriage or civil partnership certificate |
| Divorce or dissolution (reverting) | Final order or decree absolute, often with your marriage certificate |
| Any other reason | Deed poll (enrolled or unenrolled) |
| Scotland-born and recorded change | Extract from National Records of Scotland showing the recorded change |

Most banks want to see an original or a certified copy. An unenrolled deed poll is widely accepted; you don't normally need an enrolled one. If you're making your own, our [free deed poll template](/free-deed-poll-template-uk) shows the format organisations expect.

## How to Change Your Name: Step by Step

### Option 1: In Branch

1. Check your bank's website for its name change requirements and whether you need an appointment.
2. Take your original deed poll or certificate and your photo ID.
3. Bring your debit card, and any other cards or account details you want updated.
4. The staff member will check and copy your documents and hand back the originals.
5. Ask whether your savings accounts, ISAs and credit cards with the same bank will be updated at the same time, as they sometimes aren't.
6. Ask how long new cards will take to arrive.

A branch visit is often the quickest route because your originals don't leave your hands. With many branches closing, though, you may need to travel or book ahead.

### Option 2: In the App or Online

Several banks now let you request a name change through the app or online banking. You'll usually be asked to photograph or upload your evidence, and sometimes to take a selfie for identity checks. If the bank isn't satisfied with the upload, it may ask you to visit a branch or post your documents.

### Option 3: By Post

Some building societies and smaller banks handle name changes by post. You'll typically send a letter or form with an original or certified copy of your evidence. Use a tracked postal service and keep a copy of everything you send. Our [name change letters generator](/name-change-letters-generator) can help you write the covering letter.

## Cards, Cheques and Direct Debits

### New Cards

Your bank will issue new debit and credit cards in your new name. Your old cards normally keep working until the new ones are activated, but check this with your bank. Once your new card arrives, update any saved card details with online shops and subscriptions.

### Cheques

If you still use cheques, order a new cheque book. Cheques made out to your old name can usually still be paid in, especially soon after the change, but banks can refuse them, so tell anyone who pays you by cheque.

### Direct Debits and Standing Orders

These carry on as normal. Your account number and sort code don't change, so there's nothing to set up again.

---

## Joint Accounts

If you hold a joint account, the person changing their name provides their own evidence. The other account holder usually doesn't need to do anything, although some banks may ask both of you to confirm certain changes. If you're changing your name after a separation and want to close or split a joint account, that's a separate process, and many banks will need both account holders' agreement.

---

## Mortgages and Property

Tell your mortgage lender about your name change, just as you would any other lender. If you own a property, also update your name on the title register held by HM Land Registry (England and Wales), Registers of Scotland, or Land and Property Services in Northern Ireland. A mismatch between your name on the title and your other records can cause delays when you come to sell or remortgage. Check the relevant registry's website for the current process and any fee.

---

## ISAs and Savings

Changing your name has no effect on your ISA allowance or the tax-free status of your ISA. Simply tell your provider. Remember NS&I if you hold Premium Bonds, and any savings accounts you may have opened years ago and forgotten about.

---

## Your Credit File

The three main credit reference agencies in the UK are Experian, Equifax and TransUnion. They don't take name change notifications directly in the way a bank does. Instead, your credit file is updated as your lenders report your new details, and your previous name is normally kept as a linked name so your history isn't lost.

To keep things tidy:

1. Update every lender (bank, credit cards, loans, mortgage, car finance, mobile phone contract).
2. Re-register on the electoral roll in your new name through your local council.
3. After a couple of months, check your credit report with each agency. You can do this for free through their statutory or free services.
4. If something is wrong, ask the lender to correct what they report, or raise a dispute with the agency.

Avoid applying for new credit in the first few weeks after a name change, while records are still catching up. A mismatch between your application and your credit file can trigger extra checks or a decline.

## Common Problems and How to Fix Them

### Confirmation of Payee Mismatches

Confirmation of Payee is a check that compares the name someone types when paying you with the name on your account. If your employer, a friend or a business pays you using your old name after your bank has updated it, they may see a "no match" or "close match" warning. The payment can still go through, but the warning may put people off.

**Fix:** give your new name to your employer's payroll team, your landlord, HMRC and anyone else who pays you regularly. Do this at the same time as you change your bank details.

### Salary Paid to an Old Name

Your salary won't bounce simply because your name has changed, as payments go to your account number and sort code. Still, update your payroll so your payslips and bank details match.

### Inconsistent Names Across Products

Your current account might be updated but not your savings account or credit card with the same bank. Check each one and ask for any missed products to be updated.

### Old Name on Online Accounts

PayPal, Amazon and other payment services hold your name separately. Update these after your cards arrive.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. For complex situations, such as disputes over joint accounts, speak to a solicitor or Citizens Advice.

## Summary

To change your name with a UK bank, update your photo ID first, then take your deed poll, marriage certificate or divorce order to a branch, or use your bank's app or postal service if it offers one. Your account number and sort code stay the same, you'll get new cards, and your ISA allowance isn't affected. Afterwards, tell everyone who pays you so Confirmation of Payee checks match, update every lender and the electoral roll, and check your credit file with Experian, Equifax and TransUnion once things have settled.
