# How to Change Your Name After Divorce in the UK

Many people who took their spouse's surname on marriage decide to go back to their previous name after a divorce or the dissolution of a civil partnership. Others choose an entirely new name, and some keep their married name, often to share a surname with their children. All of these are legitimate choices, and in the UK you're free to make any of them.

This guide explains what evidence you'll need, when a deed poll is worth having, how to handle passports and other documents, and the rules on changing your children's surname, which is where people most often run into difficulty.

---

## Quick Overview

| Situation | Evidence Usually Needed |
| :--- | :--- |
| Reverting to maiden or previous name, divorce final | Marriage certificate plus final order or decree absolute; some organisations also want your birth certificate |
| Reverting before divorce is final | Deed poll |
| Choosing a completely new name | Deed poll |
| Double-barrelling or other variation | Deed poll |
| Changing a child's surname | Consent of everyone with parental responsibility, or a court order |

---

## Divorce Terminology Across the UK

The documents you'll be dealing with have different names depending on where and when you divorced:

- **England and Wales (divorces started from April 2022):** the conditional order is followed by the **final order**, which legally ends the marriage.
- **England and Wales (older divorces):** the decree nisi was followed by the **decree absolute**.
- **Northern Ireland:** the **decree absolute** still ends the marriage.
- **Scotland:** the court issues a decree of divorce, and you can get an **extract decree** as evidence.

For a civil partnership, the equivalent document is a final order (or dissolution order) confirming the partnership has been dissolved.

## Do You Need a Deed Poll?

### When You May Not Need One

If you're simply going back to the surname you used before your marriage, many UK organisations will accept a combination of:

1. Your marriage or civil partnership certificate (showing your previous name and your spouse's surname), and
2. Your final order or decree absolute (showing the marriage has ended).

Some will also ask for your birth certificate to show your original surname, particularly if your pre-marriage name was different from your birth name.

### Why Many People Make One Anyway

In practice, a deed poll is often worth having, for several reasons:

- **It's one clear document.** Instead of showing three documents and explaining how they link together, you show one that states your old name and your new name.
- **Some organisations insist on it.** Policies vary, and some banks or other organisations prefer a deed poll for reverting names.
- **Divorce orders can contain private information.** You may not want to hand copies of your divorce paperwork to your employer, bank and every utility company.
- **You can act before the divorce is final.** Divorces can take many months. A deed poll lets you change your name whenever you're ready.
- **It's the only route for a new name.** If you want a different surname altogether, divorce papers won't cover it.

An unenrolled deed poll is free to make yourself. You can create one using our [free deed poll generator](/change-name-in-uk-by-deedpoll).

## Changing Your Name on Your Passport

Your passport is usually the first document to update, because other organisations often want photo ID in your new name. HM Passport Office will need evidence linking your current passport name to your new name. If you're reverting to a previous name, it may accept your divorce and marriage documents, sometimes along with your birth certificate, but the exact evidence it asks for can depend on your circumstances. A deed poll is generally the most straightforward evidence.

Check the current guidance and fee on GOV.UK before applying, and don't book international travel in your new name until your new passport has arrived. Our guide to [changing your name on your passport](/how-to-change-name-on-passport-uk) explains the process step by step.

## A Practical Sequence

1. **Decide on your name.** Previous name, new name, or keep your married name.
2. **Gather your evidence.** Marriage certificate, final order or decree absolute, birth certificate, and/or a deed poll. If you're making a deed poll, sign several originals in front of an independent adult witness.
3. **Update your passport.** Apply with your evidence and wait for the new passport.
4. **Update your driving licence.** This is free and can be done online or by post. The DVLA will want to see your evidence.
5. **Tell HMRC, your employer and the DWP**, if you receive benefits.
6. **Update your bank and lenders.** If you have joint accounts or a joint mortgage with your former spouse, these may be handled as part of your financial settlement.
7. **Work through everything else.** GP, electoral register, council tax, insurers, pensions, utilities and so on.

## Children's Surnames After Divorce

### The Basic Rule

A child's surname isn't something one parent can change on their own if another person also has parental responsibility. In England and Wales, a parent who wants to change a child's surname needs the consent of everyone with parental responsibility. Where a child arrangements order is in place setting out who the child lives with, the Children Act 1989 specifically says the child can't be known by a new surname without the written consent of everyone with parental responsibility or the permission of the court.

Mothers automatically have parental responsibility. A father has it if he was married to the mother when the child was born, or, for most births registered after 1 December 2003 in England and Wales, if he's named on the birth certificate. Others can acquire it through a court order or agreement.

### If the Other Parent Agrees

If everyone with parental responsibility agrees, a child deed poll can be signed with each of them giving their consent. Our guide to [changing a child's name by deed poll](/child-deed-poll-uk) explains how this works.

### If the Other Parent Doesn't Agree

If the other parent refuses, you can apply to the family court for a **Specific Issue Order** asking for permission to change the child's surname. The court will decide based on the child's welfare, which is its paramount consideration. Courts generally treat a child's surname as an important matter and don't approve a change simply because one parent has changed their own name. Factors include the child's age and wishes, how long they've used their current name, and their relationship with each parent.

Equally, if you're worried the other parent is going to change your child's surname without your agreement, you can apply for a **Prohibited Steps Order** to stop it.

Before going to court, you'll usually need to attend a Mediation Information and Assessment Meeting (MIAM), unless an exemption applies.

### Other Options

Some families find a compromise, for example a double-barrelled surname that includes both parents' names. A child can also be known informally by a different name at school with both parents' agreement, though schools will usually want to know who holds parental responsibility.

### Scotland and Northern Ireland

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own family law. The broad principles are similar, in that parents with parental rights and responsibilities should be involved and the court looks at the child's welfare, but the procedures and terminology differ. If you live in Scotland or Northern Ireland, get local advice before taking action. In Scotland, a change of name for a child born there can be recorded with National Records of Scotland, subject to its own consent rules.

### Children Aged 16 and Over

In England and Wales, a young person aged 16 or 17 can generally change their own name by deed poll, although some organisations may still ask about parental consent.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Disputes about children's names can be sensitive, so speak to a family solicitor or Citizens Advice if you can't reach agreement.

## Summary

You can go back to your previous name after divorce at any time, and you don't have to change your name at all if you'd rather not. Once your divorce is final, your marriage certificate and final order or decree absolute are often enough evidence, but a deed poll is a cleaner, more private option and is essential if you want to change your name before the divorce is complete or choose a new name. Update your passport first, then work through your other records. If you want to change your children's surname, you'll need the agreement of everyone with parental responsibility or, failing that, a court order.
