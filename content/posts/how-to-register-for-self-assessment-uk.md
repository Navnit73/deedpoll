# How to Register for Self Assessment in the UK

Most UK employees never need to fill in a tax return, because PAYE takes care of their Income Tax automatically. But once you start earning money that isn't taxed at source — from self-employment, renting out property or certain other income — HMRC expects you to tell it, and that usually means registering for Self Assessment.

This guide explains who needs to register, how to do it, what happens next, the deadlines you can't afford to miss, and how Making Tax Digital is changing the system for sole traders and landlords. The current tax year is 2026/27, running from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027.

---

## Who Needs to File a Self Assessment Return?

You'll normally need to register and file if, in a tax year, any of these apply:

- **You're self-employed** as a sole trader and your gross income (before expenses) was more than **£1,000**, the trading allowance
- **You're a partner** in a business partnership
- **You receive rental income** of more than **£1,000** a year, the property allowance, or have rental profits HMRC can't collect through your tax code
- **You have significant untaxed income**, such as tips, commission, foreign income, or large amounts of savings or dividend income that isn't covered by your allowances
- **You need to pay the High Income Child Benefit Charge** because you or your partner receive Child Benefit and one of you has adjusted net income over £60,000
- **You have Capital Gains Tax to report** that you haven't dealt with in another way
- **HMRC sends you a notice to file**, in which case you must file even if you think you don't owe anything

### A Note on the High Income Child Benefit Charge

Historically, paying this charge meant registering for Self Assessment. HMRC has been introducing ways for employed people to pay it through their PAYE tax code instead. Check GOV.UK for the current options before registering, as you may not need a full tax return.

### If You're Not Sure

GOV.UK has a free online checker that asks a few questions and tells you whether you need to send a return. If your only extra income is small, HMRC may be able to collect the tax by adjusting your tax code instead. Our guide to [how to check your Income Tax](/tax-money/how-to-check-your-income-tax-uk) explains how to see what HMRC already knows about your income.

## The Registration Deadline: 5 October

You must register by **5 October** following the end of the tax year in which you first had income to report.

**Example:** You start freelancing in June 2026. That falls in the 2026/27 tax year, which ends on 5 April 2027. You must register by **5 October 2027**, and your first online return and payment are due by **31 January 2028**.

Registering late can lead to a penalty if it means tax is paid late, so it's best to register as soon as you know you need to.

---

## How to Register: Step by Step

The process depends on why you're registering.

### If You're Self-Employed

1. Go to the "Register for Self Assessment" page on GOV.UK.
2. Sign in with your Government Gateway user ID, or create one. You'll need an email address and your National Insurance number.
3. Choose the option to register as self-employed and give details of your business: what you do, your start date, trading name (if any) and business address.
4. Submit. This registers you for Self Assessment and for the National Insurance you'll pay on your profits.

### If You're Not Self-Employed

If you're registering because of rental income, the High Income Child Benefit Charge or other untaxed income, use the GOV.UK option for people who aren't self-employed. You'll provide your personal details and the reason you need to file.

### If You're a Partner in a Partnership

Each partner registers individually, and the partnership itself must also be registered by the nominated partner.

---

## Your UTR and Government Gateway

After registering, HMRC sends your **Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)**, a 10-digit number. It's usually sent by post and can take a few weeks, so allow time if you're registering close to the deadline. Once issued, you can also find your UTR in your personal tax account or the HMRC app.

Your **Government Gateway** account is how you'll file returns online, view your Self Assessment statement and make payments. For security, HMRC may post an activation code before you can access Self Assessment online for the first time — another reason not to leave registration until the last minute.

Keep your UTR safe. You'll need it whenever you contact HMRC about Self Assessment, and your accountant will need it if you use one. Be wary of anyone asking for it unexpectedly.

## Key Self Assessment Deadlines

| Deadline | What's due | Example for the 2026/27 tax year |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 October | Register for Self Assessment | 5 October 2027 |
| 31 October | Paper tax return | 31 October 2027 |
| 31 January | Online tax return and payment of tax owed (balancing payment) | 31 January 2028 |
| 31 January | First payment on account for the following year | 31 January 2028 |
| 31 July | Second payment on account | 31 July 2028 |

Most people file online. It gives you three more months than paper, calculates your tax automatically, and shows the result straight away.

## Payments on Account Explained

Payments on account are advance payments towards next year's bill. They catch many first-time filers off guard because, in your first year, you may have to pay **150%** of your tax bill in one go.

They normally apply if your last Self Assessment bill was **£1,000 or more**, unless more than 80% of your tax was already collected at source (for example through PAYE). Each payment is usually **half of the previous year's bill**.

### Worked Example

Chris starts self-employment in 2026/27. Chris's Income Tax and Class 4 National Insurance bill for that year comes to £4,000.

**31 January 2028:**
- Balancing payment for 2026/27: **£4,000**
- First payment on account for 2027/28 (50% of £4,000): **£2,000**
- **Total due: £6,000**

**31 July 2028:**
- Second payment on account for 2027/28: **£2,000**

If Chris's actual bill for 2027/28 turns out to be £5,000, a further £1,000 balancing payment is due on 31 January 2029, along with the first payment on account for 2028/29. If profits fall, you can apply to reduce your payments on account — but if you reduce them too far, HMRC charges interest on the shortfall.

The practical lesson: from your first month of self-employment, set aside a portion of every payment you receive. Our [National Insurance and tax calculator](/national-insurance-and-tax-calculator-uk) can help you estimate the amount.

## Penalties for Late Filing and Payment

### Late Filing (traditional Self Assessment)

| How late | Penalty |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 day | £100 fixed penalty |
| 3 months | £10 a day, up to £900 |
| 6 months | Further 5% of tax due or £300, whichever is greater |
| 12 months | Another 5% of tax due or £300, whichever is greater |

The £100 penalty applies even if you owe no tax. Larger penalties can apply where HMRC believes information was deliberately withheld.

### Late Payment

Interest is charged on tax paid late from the day after it was due, and late payment penalties can also apply. If you're within Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, a newer points-based penalty system applies. Check GOV.UK for the current rules.

If you can't pay in full, contact HMRC before the deadline. You may be able to set up a Time to Pay arrangement.

## Records to Keep

Good records make filing quicker and protect you if HMRC asks questions. Keep:

- **Sales and income records**: invoices, receipts, bank statements, platform payout reports
- **Expenses**: receipts for anything you claim, from equipment to travel
- **Property records**: rent received, letting agent statements, repair and insurance costs, mortgage interest statements
- **Other income**: P60s, P45s, P11Ds, dividend vouchers, interest statements
- **Pension contributions and Gift Aid donations**, which can affect your tax

If you're self-employed or a landlord, keep records for at least **5 years after the 31 January submission deadline** for the relevant tax year. Otherwise, keep them for at least 22 months after the end of the tax year.

## Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax changes how sole traders and landlords report income. Instead of one annual return, you keep digital records using compatible software, send quarterly updates to HMRC, and submit a final declaration after the tax year.

| From | Who must join |
| :--- | :--- |
| April 2026 | Sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 |
| April 2027 | Sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £30,000 |

"Qualifying income" means your combined gross self-employment and property income — turnover, not profit. Your employment wages and pension aren't included. The government has indicated it intends to bring more people in over time, so check GOV.UK for the latest thresholds.

If you're registering for Self Assessment for the first time and expect income near these thresholds, choose MTD-compatible software from the start.

## How to Stop Self Assessment

If you no longer need to file — for example, you've stopped trading, sold your rental property, or your untaxed income is now dealt with through your tax code — tell HMRC through GOV.UK. If you're self-employed, you'll be asked for the date you stopped trading.

You'll usually still need to file a final return covering the period up to when you stopped. If HMRC has already sent a notice to file for a year, you must submit that return or ask HMRC to withdraw the notice — ignoring it can still lead to penalties.

For complex situations, such as several businesses, foreign income or large capital gains, it's worth speaking to an accountant or HMRC.

---

## Summary

If you earn more than £1,000 from self-employment or property, have other untaxed income, or HMRC asks you to, you'll need to register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year ends. Register through GOV.UK with your Government Gateway account, wait for your UTR, and file online by 31 January. Budget for payments on account, which can mean paying 150% of your bill in your first January. Keep records for at least five years, check whether Making Tax Digital applies to you, and tell HMRC promptly when you no longer need to file. Do these things and Self Assessment becomes a routine annual task rather than a source of penalties.
