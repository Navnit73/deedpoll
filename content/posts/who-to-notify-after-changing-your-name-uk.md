# Who to Notify After Changing Your Name in the UK

Making a deed poll is the easy part. The real work starts afterwards, when you have to get every organisation that holds your details to update its records. Do it in a sensible order and it's mostly paperwork. Do it in the wrong order and you can end up with a bank that won't accept your new name because your ID is still in the old one, or a flight booking that doesn't match your passport.

This guide gives you a practical order to work through, explains what each organisation usually asks for, and covers the mistakes that most often slow people down. If you haven't made your deed poll yet, you can create one with our [free deed poll generator](/change-name-in-uk-by-deedpoll).

## Before You Start: Get Your Paperwork in Order

### Sign Several Originals

An unenrolled deed poll is a document you sign in front of a witness. There's nothing stopping you from signing several identical copies at the same time, each with its own original signatures. Each one is then an original deed poll in its own right.

Many people sign between five and ten. This lets you send one to HM Passport Office and another to the DVLA while still having originals to show your bank in person. Keep them flat, in a folder, and never laminate them.

### Know the Difference Between Originals and Certified Copies

- **Original**: a copy with the original wet-ink signatures of you and your witness.
- **Certified copy**: a photocopy that a solicitor or other suitable professional has signed and dated to confirm it's a true copy of the original.

Government departments usually want originals and send them back. Many private organisations accept certified copies, and some only need to see the original in a branch.

### Set Up a Tracking Spreadsheet

A simple spreadsheet saves a lot of confusion. Use these columns:

1. Organisation name
2. How you contacted them (online, phone, post, branch)
3. Date contacted
4. Documents sent (and whether originals or copies)
5. Reference number
6. Date confirmed updated
7. Date originals returned

This makes it easy to chase anything that has gone missing, and it means you'll know where every original deed poll is.

## Stage 1: Your Photo ID

### Passport

Your passport is usually the most important document to update first, because many other organisations will want photo ID in your new name. HM Passport Office will normally ask for your current passport and evidence of your name change, such as an original deed poll or marriage certificate. You'll need to pay the standard passport fee, so check the current fee on GOV.UK before applying.

One important point: don't book international travel in your new name until the new passport arrives. Airlines and border control check that your booking matches your passport. Our guide on [how to change your name on your passport](/how-to-change-name-on-passport-uk) covers the process in detail.

### Driving Licence

You're required to keep your driving licence details up to date, and changing your name on it is free. You can apply online or by post, sending your licence and evidence of the change. If you need to send a new photo, a fee may apply in some cases, so check GOV.UK. See our step-by-step guide to [changing your name on your driving licence](/change-name-on-driving-licence-dvla-uk).

### Vehicle Log Book (V5C)

If you're the registered keeper of a vehicle, update the name on your V5C as well. This is separate from your licence. Fill in the relevant section of the log book and send it to the DVLA. There's no fee for this.

## Stage 2: Tax, Work and Benefits

### HMRC

Your employer's payroll will eventually pass your new name to HMRC, but it's better to update HMRC directly. You can usually do this through your personal tax account, or by calling the Income Tax helpline on 0300 200 3300. Your National Insurance number doesn't change when your name does. If you also have a tax code or refund question, raise it at the same time.

### Employer and Payroll

Tell your HR or payroll team. They'll usually want to see evidence and may take a copy for your personnel file. Ask them to update your payslip, email address, staff ID card and any pension scheme they run. Your first payslip in your new name is a good check that everything has gone through.

### DWP and Benefits

If you receive Universal Credit, report the change through your online journal. For other benefits such as State Pension, Child Benefit, PIP or Carer's Allowance, contact the office that pays the benefit. Each benefit may be handled by a different team, so tell each one.

### DBS Certificates

An existing DBS certificate will still show your old name. If you work in a role that requires DBS checks, tell your employer. When you next apply, you'll need to list all previous names, so keep your deed poll handy.

### Professional Bodies

If you're registered with a regulator or professional body (for example in medicine, nursing, law, accountancy, teaching or social work), tell them promptly. Your registration name often needs to match the name you practise under, and some regulators have their own forms and evidence requirements.

## Stage 3: Money

### Banks and Building Societies

Most banks will update your name once they've seen your evidence and ID. Some let you start online or in their app, while others need a branch visit. Ask for new cards and, if you still use them, a new cheque book.

### Credit Cards, Loans and Mortgages

Each lender holds your name separately, so tell each one. If you own property, you should also update the name on the Land Registry title so it matches your other records.

### Pensions

Contact every pension provider, including old workplace schemes. Mismatched names are a common reason for delays when people come to draw their pension years later.

### Investments, ISAs and Premium Bonds

Update your ISA provider, any investment platforms and NS&I if you hold Premium Bonds. A name change doesn't affect your ISA allowance.

### Student Loans Company

Update your details through your online student loan account. If you're repaying through PAYE, your employer and HMRC records should match too.

## Stage 4: Health, Home and Everyday Accounts

### GP and NHS

Tell your GP practice. They'll update your NHS record, which then flows through to other NHS services. Your NHS number doesn't change. Also tell your dentist, optician and any hospital clinic you're under.

### Electoral Register

Register again in your new name through your local council. This matters more than people expect: lenders and credit reference agencies use the electoral roll to confirm identity.

### Council Tax

Update your council tax account online through your local council's website.

### Utilities, Broadband and Mobile

Gas, electricity, water, broadband, TV and mobile providers rarely ask for evidence. Most changes can be made through your online account.

### Insurers

Tell your car, home, travel, pet and life insurers. Incorrect details could cause problems if you need to claim. For car insurance, make sure the name matches your driving licence.

### Everything Else

- TV Licensing
- Schools, colleges and universities
- Gym, clubs and memberships
- Loyalty schemes and online shopping accounts
- Your solicitor, if you have a will (consider updating it)
- Vets and pet microchip databases

## Differences Across the UK

The list above applies throughout the UK, since most of these organisations are UK-wide. The main difference is how you evidence your new name. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, a deed poll is the usual route. In Scotland, deed polls are less commonly used; people born or adopted in Scotland can apply to National Records of Scotland to have a change of name recorded, and many organisations will accept that evidence instead.

## Common Problems and How to Avoid Them

1. **Updating your bank before your ID.** Some banks need ID in your new name. Do your passport or licence first.
2. **Running out of originals.** Sign enough originals at the start.
3. **Booking travel too early.** Always book in the name on the passport you'll travel with.
4. **Forgetting old pensions.** Dig out old paperwork and contact every scheme.
5. **Not checking the result.** Ask for written confirmation or check your online account to make sure the change has gone through.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. If your situation is complicated, speak to a solicitor or Citizens Advice.

## Summary

Work through your name change in stages: photo ID first, then tax, work and benefits, then money, then everyday accounts. Sign several original deed polls so you can deal with more than one organisation at once, keep a spreadsheet of who you've contacted and where your originals are, and don't book travel in your new name until your passport arrives. Following this order means each organisation can see the evidence it needs, and the whole process is far less likely to stall.
